# Problem Statement: Inkloom AI

> Transcribed from `Problem_statement.pdf` (5 pages; text layer cross-checked with OCR).

## The problem

Growing organisations publish content through multiple employees, agencies and AI tools, making it difficult to maintain a consistent and trustworthy brand identity.

Incorrect claims, outdated information, inconsistent language and off-brand visuals can damage credibility before anyone notices the problem.

Build an **intelligent brand-governance platform** that reviews content **before publication** and checks it against approved guidelines, facts and assets.

The system should detect issues involving **tone, terminology, messaging, visual consistency, accessibility and unsupported promotional claims**.

Instead of simply rejecting content, it should **explain each issue** and **suggest corrections that preserve the creator's original purpose**.

Include a **review and approval workflow** through which creators, managers and authorised reviewers can collaborate and track changes.

The product should support **different content formats** and provide **insights into repeated mistakes or areas where the brand guidelines are unclear**.

The final solution must **balance automation with human control** while **protecting confidential materials** and producing **reliable, explainable results**.

## Expected outcome

A functional product that helps an organisation prevent brand inconsistency before content is published. It should use approved source material, provide understandable findings, support human review and demonstrate value across realistic content workflows.

## Suggested product capabilities

- Import or define brand guidelines, approved facts, terminology and reusable assets.
- Review text, images, documents or other supported content formats before publication.
- Detect inconsistent tone, language, facts, claims, visual usage and accessibility issues.
- Explain why an issue was flagged and identify the guideline or approved source involved.
- Suggest corrections without automatically overriding important human decisions.
- Support comments, approvals, revision history and role-based review workflows.
- Identify recurring problems and provide useful governance insights over time.
- Protect confidential brand materials and clearly disclose the use of AI or external services.

## Submission requirements

An event may use one or more submission stages. Participants must complete every item announced for the stage that applies to their event.

### Round 1: The Plan
- Problem definition and evidence showing that the problem is real.
- Target users, their needs and the organisational setting in which the product will be used.
- Research, interviews, observations, surveys or other supporting evidence.
- Proposed solution, value proposition and end-to-end product workflow.
- Planned features, technology stack, integrations and data sources.
- Responsible-design considerations, including privacy, security and human oversight.
- Development plan, role allocation and expected impact.

### Round 2: The Build
- A functional product, prototype or demonstrable MVP.
- A public or judge-accessible source-code repository.
- A deployed link, installation package or clear functional demonstration.
- Product architecture and an explanation of the main technical decisions.
- Details of APIs, AI models, datasets, libraries, templates and external services used.
- Evidence of user testing or structured feedback where reasonably available.
- A clear statement of each team member's contribution.

### Round 3: The Finish
- The final working product and source-code repository.
- A product demo video or live demonstration, as requested.
- A concise presentation or report covering problem, users, solution, workflow, technology and impact.
- Evidence of testing, reliability improvements and changes made during development.
- A privacy, security, accessibility and responsible-design explanation.
- Known limitations and a realistic future roadmap.
- Complete participant and contribution details in the required format.

## Rules that affect how we work

1. Product must be built **during the official build window**; earlier work doesn't count and substantial pre-built work can be disqualified (rules 1, 4). Research, planning and interviews beforehand are allowed (rule 2).
2. Commit history / file metadata may be inspected (rule 3), so commit small and often, from each member's own account.
3. Libraries, APIs, open source allowed per licence (rule 5). Templates/boilerplate must be disclosed (rule 6).
4. AI / no-code tools allowed, but **significant tools and AI-generated components must be disclosed** (rule 7, Submission Integrity).
5. Each member must state their own role and contribution accurately (rules 14–16).
6. Respect licences, IP, data protection and privacy (rule 10).
