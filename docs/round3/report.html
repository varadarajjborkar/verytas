Section 1

01At a glance

Verytas is a review gate between a finished draft and publishing. A creator submits a post; Verytas finds and explains each problem with the rule or fact behind it and suggests the smallest fix; the brand team approves or sends it back, and every step is recorded. In Round 3 we delivered the three improvements we committed to, with one part left out on purpose and explained, and spent the rest of the time on reliability.

GitHub Actions

Open verytas.pages.dev, pick a person at Kitepath (a made-up online learning brand with eight weeks of history) and follow the short tour. Creator and brand team are one click apart through Switch account.

Try it in five steps

  1. As Dev Sharma (creator), open My reviews, paste "Kitepath has 2 million learners. Guaranteed results!" and run the review. The figure is checked against the approved fact (1.2 million) and the promise is flagged, each with its rule, a fix and a confidence.
  2. Apply the fixes and send it for approval. A reading you disagree with can be disputed with a reason, or escalated to a person.
  3. Switch account to Arjun Mehta (brand team). The dashboard opens on what changed since you were last here; the piece is in the review queue, ranked by what needs attention first.
  4. Talk it through. Open Conversations beside the piece: messages, files, and a Meet, Teams or Zoom link that becomes a Join card. Ask @Inki about the record once both sides agree.
  5. Decide. Approve, or send it back with the fixes asked for. Back as Dev, the news is already on the dashboard and the badge, without a reload.

The review screen: each issue marked in place, ranked must fix to consider, with the rule behind it, the suggested fix and the steps from written to published.

Section 2

02The three improvements

What we committed to at the end of Round 2, what was built, and the one part of each we chose not to build, with the reason.

1

Creators and managers talk it through

Committed: one-on-one meetings between a creator and their manager by chat, voice or video, with only the time, the way and an outcome recorded against the reviews.

Built: Conversations beside each piece, laid out as an inbox: chat bubbles for both sides, replies, edits and deletes, files, unread counts and live delivery. A Google Meet, Teams, Zoom, Skype, Jitsi or Whereby link becomes a Join card, so a call starts in one click on a free app people already use. Messages are encrypted at rest. @Inki answers questions about the record once the asker agrees to it reading the thread, and recalls the earlier turns a question depends on. The thread itself, with its times, sits beside the piece it is about, so it is the meeting's record and needs no separate log.

Not built, on purpose: voice through masked temporary numbers. Telephony is separate, paid infrastructure (number rental, per-minute charges, carrier rules); for a prototype it is costly and adds little over the meeting apps both sides already have.

2

Sharper vision and speech

Committed: recoloured icons caught by separating shape from colour, a Hindi and Hinglish speech model, a GPU that wakes on demand, and before and after on the same benchmarks.

Built:

  • Recoloured app icons are now found by their outline when the colours are swapped: 33% to 100% (24 of 24), and artwork misuse overall from 92.9% to 96.0% with false alarms unchanged at 4.7%.
  • Mascots: the segmentation cut-out is checked and repaired before the comparison, and a stretched body is named. False alarms on acceptable characters 64 to 50 of 180; stretched bodies caught 0 to 18 of 30.
  • A vision worker that wakes on demand: the mascot models run on a second instance that starts when a review needs it and stops after 20 idle minutes. In judging hours (9 AM to 9 PM IST, 31 Oct to 13 Nov) a timer keeps it awake under a hard 10 USD cap, so a judge does not wait for a boot.
  • Before and after are published in backend/evals/results/assets.md and mascots.md, on the same scenes as Round 2.

Not built, and why: the GPU. AWS refused our GPU quota request twice, so every model runs on CPU or on the remote service as before. The stronger Hindi speech model needed that GPU to run in reasonable time and was dropped with it; codenames said aloud in Hindi are still caught by matching their sound.

3

The Brand Console runs it

Committed: an approved-people tab with agreement dates, a video timeline with findings as marks, and managers marking a video moment or an image area with comments the creator sees in place.

Built, all of it:

  • Approved people in the Library: who may appear in content, the kind of agreement, its channels and dates, private notes and the reference photos faces are matched against. Each card says where the agreement stands today; dates extend and agreements turn off in place.
  • Video in the review: each finding is a mark on the timeline at its moment; picking it there or in the list seeks to it. Drag along the timeline to comment on a stretch; speed runs from 0.5x to 2x. No download, picture in picture or casting.
  • Image markup: a marker in four thicknesses, an eraser and text labels, saved with a written comment. Pinned comments and their drawings show in place for both sides.

Conversations: the brand team's inbox, and a thread with a creator about a piece, with a Google Meet link shown as a Join card.

Section 5

05Architecture

Two web apps share one codebase and one API. Prism, the review engine, looks at what a submission contains and spawns an agent for each part. Rules run in code first; models only judge meaning, and only after confidential terms are removed.

How Prism checks a post

Prism is orchestration written in code, not a model choosing its own tools, so the same input always gets the same checks and a post cannot talk it into skipping one. A screenshot pasted on page 3 of a brochure gets a document agent, a page agent, an image agent, an OCR agent and then a text agent, which runs the same three workers as a typed caption: rule checks (code), a claim verifier (claims against dated facts, numbers compared in code) and a semantic judge (yes or no per rule, with a probability). Agents post the figures, artwork and people they find to a shared evidence board, and a cross-check agent compares the parts. Context layers then read the whole post, and the creator's note is weighed last. Depth and agent counts are capped; anything cut off, timed out or failed is shown as not checked, never as passed.

Key decisions

DecisionWhy
Rules before modelsFacts, words, codenames and artwork are checked in code. Only a model-free check can block a post; a model's reading caps at a warning, because content can be written to sway a model.
Media stays localOCR, speech, PDF rendering and artwork matching run on our CPU; only redacted text and pixels reach a model.
Pull-based deploysThe server pulls a branch only green CI can move, builds beside the live release, health-checks it and rolls back on failure.
One cheap serverOne small ARM instance in Mumbai behind a free edge, a vision worker that sleeps, and a hard AWS budget cap.

Section 7

07Testing and reliability

Round 3 asks that it survive bad input, empty states, a refresh and a double click, and that a stranger can finish the main journey and run it from the README. This is how we checked each.

CheckWhat it covers, and the result
Automated tests785 backend tests, offline with no keys, on every push, with type checks, lint and a production build of the web app, a secret scan and dependency audits. Main only moves when all of it is green.
Bad input on every routeA test sends every API route malformed input as every role and fails on any server error; ids past 64 bits, oversized files and wrong types answer with a 4xx and a sentence.
Black-box simulatorKnows Verytas only through its public API and steers each round toward what it misses: 96% recall, 0% false alarms on clean posts, 0 server errors at 12 parallel submissions (17 before), and 320 cases at 32 in parallel run clean.
Reviewed before commitEvery batch of changes was reviewed for bugs and security issues before it was committed. In Round 3 that raised 217 findings: 212 fixed, 3 kept on purpose with a reason, 2 declined.
The live site as every personEvery page loaded and refreshed as each demo person: no console errors, no server errors, no error screens. A double click makes one piece, a refresh mid-review resumes, and empty pages say why and what to do next.
Phone widthA pass over every page at 390 px, fixed where anything overflowed or was cut off.
Speed on the live siteAPI reads 63 to 114 ms (the network alone is about 60 ms), pages settle in 0.2 to 0.7 s, reviews take 6 to 23 s, a message reaches the other side in about 0.1 s and a decision in about 0.2 s.
Clone and runA fresh clone of the public repository, with no keys, followed the README to a running API, web app and seeded demo.
Tried by peopleThree first-time users used the live demo on their own. Every point they raised has a shipped change: the tour, plain-word help on every status, "how it was checked" on every result, progress while a review runs, next steps on empty pages (docs/research/user-testing.md).

Failures we found and fixed in Round 3

  • Unreadable messages after a demo reset: a cache outlived the reset and served pages sealed with the old key. Cached pages are now keyed by the message key's fingerprint and served only when they match the latest message.
  • A cleared finding came back when the creator ran the check again; the brand team's decision is now carried onto the same issue.
  • Slow reads under load: readers took SQLite's write lock; p99 fell from 850 ms to 53 ms once they stopped.
  • A fresh clone showed sample data: the clean-clone check found that the seed wrote the demo tokens but not the API address, so the web app fell back to its labelled sample data. The seed now writes the address too, and a test covers it.
Section 8

08Security and responsible AI

A security review ran on the code and the live site; each finding is fixed with a regression test or listed as open in docs/security/README.md.

AreaHow it is held
Signed and loggedEvery finding is signed (Ed25519) and every action goes into a SHA-256 hash-chained audit log, so an edit shows.
Confidential termsRedacted from text and blacked out in images before any model sees them; matched in part and by sound.
Hostile inputFile types read from the bytes, Office files parsed with entity expansion off and every part capped, documents converted in a sealed service with no network, web pages rendered with no scripts, and instructions aimed at the AI refused.
Conversations R3Messages sealed at rest with AES-GCM; a creator sees only their own threads; Inki answers only on consent, sees only that thread and pieces both may open, and has an hourly limit.
Ask Inki R3Lookups run as the asker, confidential tier left out, arguments never un-redacted, at most 4 rounds and 600 lookups an hour; a request for its instructions gets a fixed answer.
Memory R3Only from a person's own Ask Inki chats, only they can list or forget it, and forgetting removes it from disk (checked by a test).
MCP tokens R3Stored hashed, refused by the REST API, read and submit only, capped at internal clearance and rate limited.
People in chargeA model-only finding never blocks; every override needs a written reason; every page says when a check was degraded or not run.
Section 9

09Known limits

What a judge may run into, stated plainly.

AreaWhat to expect
Generated benchmarksEvery figure in Section 6 comes from generated or hand-labelled sets, some of which thresholds were set on. Real traffic will score lower.
Mascots on busy backgroundsThe cut-out still takes in background, so 50 of 180 acceptable scenes raise a false alarm (21 with a perfect cut-out). A vision opinion alone never blocks a post.
Hindi speechTranscribed with much error on CPU. Codenames are caught by sound; other Hindi claims less reliably.
Long threadsA decision changed late in a long conversation reaches Inki in about half to two thirds of hard cases.
No voice calls inside VerytasCalls run on Meet, Teams or Zoom through Join cards (Section 2).
Post numbersTyped or imported from a sheet, not read from the platforms.
SpeedModels run on a remote service without our own GPU. Text takes seconds; images, documents and video take longer. If the service is down, those checks show as degraded, not passed.
Shared demo peopleEvery judge signs in as the same sample people, so you may see what other visitors submitted. Ask Inki's memory for them is per browser and lapses after a day.
Your own workspace starts emptyA new company has no rules, facts or artwork until some are added or a brand book is handed to Intake. The sample company shows everything working.

10Roadmap

In the order we would build it, each with the measurement that says whether it worked.

#NextWhy, and how we would know
1A GPU once AWS grants the quotaRuns the language, vision and Hindi speech models ourselves: faster reviews, content that never leaves our servers, and a Hindi speech model. Measured on the same speech and video benchmarks.
2A better segmenter for mascotsMost remaining mascot false alarms come from the cut-out; a perfect one would take them from 50 to 21 of 180.
3Performance from the platformsInstagram's insights first, so live numbers no longer need typing, and Guideline health compares rules on real reach.
4Measured on real trafficA pilot brand's decided reviews become the benchmark, replacing generated sets, and calibration learns from real reviewers.
5Where people already workSlack and email notifications, a CMS hook that holds a post until it is approved, and single sign-on.
6Scale outPostgres (the code is ready for it) and more than one API worker behind the same Redis, once one server is no longer enough.
